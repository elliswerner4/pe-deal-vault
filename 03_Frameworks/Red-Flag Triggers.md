# Red-Flag Triggers

The 10 stop-and-check patterns. When one fires mid-analysis, stop and resolve it (or escalate it) before continuing — asking the wrong question late wastes more time than asking the right question now. These override the default "research first, then ask" cadence because they are pattern-recognition shortcuts that materially change the deal — and asking the wrong question late wastes more time than asking the right question now.

---

## Trigger 1 — QofE Addback >15% of Reported EBITDA

**Why it fires.** Addbacks compounding usually signal recurring "one-times." When management's adjusted EBITDA is materially higher than reported (more than 15%), the addbacks themselves become the analytical question, not just the diligence outcome.

**Ask the user.** "Management's adjusted EBITDA is $X.X — about XX% higher than reported. The addbacks include [list 3-5 largest]. Are any of these patterns we've seen repeat in prior years' financials? Want me to dig into the addback breakdown before continuing?"

**What it changes.** Bid range narrows; QofE rejection probability rises; specific indemnity asks form.

---

## Trigger 2 — Top Customer >20% of Gross Profit (not revenue)

**Why it fires.** Revenue concentration is a known PE filter (top-1 >10% yellow). The deeper risk is concentration of *EBITDA*. Per Strategex CDD research, often 5-10% of customers generate >50% of EBITDA even when no single customer exceeds 10% of revenue. This is a structuring conversation, not a footnote.

**Ask the user.** "Top customer represents >20% of gross profit (not just revenue). This is a structuring conversation — earnout vs. indemnity vs. walk. Want me to model how the bid math changes under different CoC retention assumptions?"

**What it changes.** Bid caps at 8-9× until earnout structure mitigates; specific R&W carve-out for CoC terminations.

---

## Trigger 3 — CFO Tenure <2 Years and No Prior PE Exit

**Why it fires.** Portfolio CFO turnover and sponsor dissatisfaction with first-year portfolio CFOs are widely reported in practitioner surveys (Odgers Interim has published the most-cited stats, though sample size and methodology should be treated as directional rather than precise). A CFO who hasn't been through a PE process is a different operating-system risk regardless of the exact replacement rate.

**Ask the user.** "CFO has been in seat <2 years with no prior PE-backed exit experience. Should I underwrite an 18-month replacement scenario in the operating model? And recommend a retention package design in the IC memo?"

**What it changes.** Operating model carries CFO transition friction; retention package goes into IC memo recommendations; succession map becomes a key memo exhibit.

---

## Trigger 4 — Capex/D&A <0.8× for 3+ Years

**Why it fires.** Persistent underinvestment is deferred maintenance. Per Disruptive Process Solutions analysis, emergency repairs average several multiples of planned maintenance cost. If a business consistently spends well below depreciation on capex, current EBITDA likely overstates a true normalized number. (Most QofE practitioners use 1.0× as the rough peg and flag anything <0.8× sustained.)

**Ask the user.** "Capex has been ~$X.XM vs. depreciation $X.XM for three years running — the ratio of XX% suggests deferred maintenance. Want me to commission technical DD to quantify catch-up capex, or run a sensitivity case on hidden capex impact?"

**What it changes.** Catch-up capex in base-case operating model years 1-2; technical DD scope explicit.

---

## Trigger 5 — Sell-Side QofE Firm Was Prior Auditor / Recently Engaged Advisor

**Why it fires.** Independence concern. The sell-side QofE is supposed to be a third-party check on management's adjusted EBITDA; if the QofE firm has a prior relationship with the target, the addbacks may be more aggressive than a truly independent firm would have allowed.

**Ask the user.** "The sell-side QofE is by [firm], which was the target's auditor through [year] (or recently advised on [matter]). Should I anticipate adjusting the QofE-adjusted EBITDA downward in our underwrite, and frame this as the rationale for our QofE retainer to verify?"

**What it changes.** Re-anchor underwrite EBITDA below sell-side number; buyer-side QofE budget increased.

---

## Trigger 6 — Reimbursement Code Concentration >30% in One CPT/HCPCS/J-Code

**Why it fires.** Healthcare services regulatory single-point-of-failure. CMS rule changes hit specific codes hardest (V28 risk adjustment, CY2026 HOPPS site-neutral shifts, CY2026 Home Health PPS). A business with >30% revenue from one code is exposed to single-rule decisions.

**Ask the user.** "Target has >30% revenue concentration in one [CPT / HCPCS / J-code]. The relevant CMS rule activity for that code is [recent / pending]. Want me to underwrite a -200 to -500bps reimbursement haircut as base case, and quantify the bear case?"

**What it changes.** Operating model carries explicit reimbursement haircut; bear-case scenario centers on this code's outcome.

---

## Trigger 7 — Entry Multiple >1.5× Sector 10-Year Median

**Why it fires.** Per Bain/MSCI, exit-to-entry spread compressed from ~4 points (2010-2020) to 1.6 points starting 2020; 2022-Q3 2024 GPs exited *below* held-portfolio marks. Buying at >1.5× sector 10-year median entry multiple bakes in multiple expansion that empirically isn't happening.

**Ask the user.** "Asking multiple is X.X× vs. sector 10-year median of Y.Y× (X% premium). Bid math needs the bull case AND no diligence surprises AND multiple expansion at exit, which compressed materially in 2022+. Should we pass, or want me to model the flat-multiple-exit IRR sensitivity?"

**What it changes.** Bid recommendation flips to walk-away above threshold; bull case becomes the only path to clearing hurdle.

---

## Trigger 8 — SOC 2 Type II Lapsed / No MFA on Admin / Prior Ransom Payment in Bank Records

**Why it fires.** Post-Change Healthcare (Feb 2024, 190M PHI breach, $2.9B remediation), cyber is a deal-killer category. The Change Healthcare attack vector was elementary: a Citrix portal without MFA. SOC 2 Type II is now baseline for B2B targets. Bank records revealing prior crypto/Bitcoin payments may indicate undisclosed prior ransom.

**Ask the user.** "[Specific finding triggered]. Cyber RWI carve-out and remediation as closing condition are the standard mitigations. Should I instruct the tech DD subagent to commission an independent pen test and dark-web exposure scan? Want me to model remediation capex impact on year-1 EBITDA?"

**What it changes.** Cyber DD subagent invoked immediately; RWI carve-out; remediation as CP; closing-conditions list expanded.

---

## Trigger 9 — Material Customer Contract with Change-of-Control Termination at "Sole Discretion"

**Why it fires.** Per Potomac Law and Tech Contracts, CoC consent clauses present in 60-70% of tech deals. Customer can terminate at sole discretion = extortionate renegotiation risk at signing.

**Ask the user.** "Top [N] customer contracts (representing XX% of revenue) have change-of-control termination at the customer's sole discretion. This is a deal term, not a diligence finding. Want me to assess the pre-signing consent-campaign feasibility, or build the indemnity carve-out into the bid structure?"

**What it changes.** This becomes a deal structure conversation. Pre-signing consent campaign or escrow / indemnity carve-out.

---

## Trigger 10 — Multi-Employer Pension Plan Participation + Union Workforce >20%

**Why it fires.** Multi-employer pension withdrawal liability is often 5-10× annual contribution and is a frequent post-close 8-figure surprise. Critical for legacy industrial deals.

**Ask the user.** "Target participates in [N] multi-employer pension plans with union workforce of XX% of headcount. Withdrawal liability is often 5-10× annual contribution. Want me to quantify the exposure via actuary engagement before LOI signing, and recommend a debt-like adjustment to the net debt schedule?"

**What it changes.** Net debt schedule explicitly itemizes withdrawal liability; actuary engaged pre-LOI; seller indemnity asks targeted.

---

## How the agent handles a trigger

1. **Stop the current research workflow.**
2. **State the trigger explicitly** to the user (which trigger fired, what the underlying signal was, with citation).
3. **Ask the question** (offer 2-4 discrete options where they exist; plain open-ended question otherwise).
4. **Wait for response** before continuing.
5. **Update the memo's risk matrix and open-question list** with the trigger's mitigation.

If multiple triggers fire on the same deal, surface them all at once with a single user prompt — don't interrupt repeatedly.
